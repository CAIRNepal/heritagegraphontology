TGDK Review — Evaluation Section of "HeritageGraph"
Reviewer stance: adversarial; recommend rejection unless the evaluation supports every scientific claim.

PART 1 — Does the evaluation validate the claimed contributions?
1. Event-mediated heritage state modelling — △ Partially validated. Evidence: CQ1/CQ4/CQ8 execute; SHACL catches a Temple without style; the Kasthamandap listing shows two events on one object. What is missing: any evidence that event mediation is better than static attachment. Every CQ retrieves asserted triples from a 277-triple ABox authored by the same team; nothing shows a query that a static-property model could not answer, or temporal reasoning over successive assessments (CQ8's own motivation). Add: an ablation — model the same Kasthamandap facts with static properties and show which CQs (condition-at-time, conflicting dates) become unanswerable or lossy.
   [FIXED 2026-07-21] Ablation implemented and run: `evaluation/ablation_event_vs_static.py` flattens the same Kasthamandap facts onto the object and poses four paired questions to both graphs. Result: event-mediated 4/4 answered, static baseline 1/4; degraded variants show the baseline retrieves bare values but loses time-spans, per-claim sources, confidence, and claim-to-agent attribution. Reported in main.tex §7.3, paragraph "Ablation: event mediation versus static attachment."

2. Living Goddess lifecycle — △ Partially validated. CQ26–32 pass and SHACL catches a tenure without a time-span. But the paper's central theoretical claim — anti-rigidity, and the inadequacy of actor–role modelling — is never operationalised. The exactly-one-deity axiom is only ever exercised by SHACL, never by DL reasoning. Add: an inconsistency-injection test: assert two owl:differentFrom deities on one tenure and show HermiT derives inconsistency. That is the cheapest possible proof that the OWL axioms bite rather than decorate.
   [FIXED 2026-07-21] Two-arm injection test implemented and run: `evaluation/inconsistency_injection.py`. Reported in main.tex §7.1. Note for reproducibility: the injected ABox must declare hg:embodiedDeity an owl:ObjectProperty, otherwise the OWLAPI parser reads the triples as annotations and HermiT ignores them.

PART 3 — Competency Questions
Executed with rdflib without reasoning, every CQ is answered from asserted triples. That means the evaluation demonstrates retrieval, not semantic capability: not a single answer depends on subsumption, inverse expansion, or property-hierarchy inference. 27 of 32 CQs return exactly one row; the maximum is 4.

Genuinely structural CQs (multi-pattern joins): CQ2 (component + ritual), CQ8 (ritual + condition assessment), CQ9 (caste + ritual criticality + festival), CQ24/CQ28 (cross-tradition joins), CQ32 (lifecycle termination). Trivial single-BGP retrieval: CQ1, CQ3, CQ5, CQ6, CQ11, CQ19, CQ25, CQ26, CQ27, CQ31, most of the rest.

Stronger design, cheap to run: execute all 32 CQs twice — over asserted triples and over the OWL-RL closure — and report which CQs gain answers only under entailment (e.g., a CQ phrased against RitualEvent answered by a Festival-typed individual, or one traversing an inverse asserted only one way). If no CQ changes, that is itself an important negative finding about how much the axiomatisation contributes.
   [FIXED 2026-07-22] Exactly this design implemented as `evaluation/cq_entailment.py` (see PART 2 §7.3 fix above): 31/32 unchanged (the negative finding is stated in §7.3 as such), CQ18 gains a row via owl:inverseOf. The script also emits the structural census (2–8 patterns, median 3; 14/32 multi-pattern) now reported in §7.3's intro, replacing the unqualified pass-rate claim.

PART 8 — Yale LUX Migration
What it demonstrates: the mappings are executable against third-party data at scale — a real and uncommon strength. What it does not demonstrate: interoperability of the contribution. The 32,342 migrated records land in five generic classes (Person, Group, Place, HumanMadeObject, Set) — not one touches the four novel patterns; the migrated graph exercises exactly the object-centric modelling the paper argues is insufficient. The "100%" figure is class-type coverage; only 3.6% of typed instances are actually migrated, and the selection criterion for those 32,342 is not explained. The experiment validates the mappings, not the ontology.

Missing evidence: (a) do the migrated records pass HeritageGraph's SHACL shapes? (unreported — and note Temple requires hasArchitecturalStyle, so any record migrating into the pattern classes would likely fail); (b) can any of the 32 CQs be answered over migrated data? (c) round-trip fidelity. Also fix the 438 vs. 455 triple-count contradiction between §7.6 and §7.7.

PART 9 — Threats to Validity

CQ circularity — the CQs, their SPARQL translations, and the ABox witnesses were all authored by the same team; the ABox was explicitly populated to make all 32 pass.
Near-vacuous consistency — with one disjointness axiom and no domains, the consistency result has little discriminative power.
No comparative baseline — the abstract claims "no existing ontology resolves [the four challenges] together," but no experiment models even one scenario in CIDOC-CRM alone or ArCo for comparison.
Enumeration bypass — the paper showcases 51 URI-carrying named individuals and SKOS schemes, yet the demonstrator and shapes use plain string literals ("Equivalence", "TempleGuthi", "NityaPuja"). The published enumeration individuals are not what instance data uses; this is unmentioned anywhere.
Multi-calendar model untested (see Part 1.6).
Promissory results — the in-progress LUX population run is not evidence and should be moved to future work or completed.

Recommendation: Major Revision.

The resource is real, carefully engineered, and unusually well instrumented — the scripted protocol, full-pass mapping audit, honest open-shapes disclosure, and the contradiction experiment are above the TGDK median. But the evaluation currently establishes that the ontology is consistent, populated, retrievable, and mapped — not that its four patterns are necessary, load-bearing, or superior to existing practice, which is what the abstract claims. The consistency result is nearly unfalsifiable, the CQ suite is self-fulfilling by construction, the multi-calendar contribution is entirely unevaluated, the provenance experiment does not isolate added value over plain PROV-O, and the paper contradicts itself on core metrics between abstract, introduction, evaluation, and conclusion. Experiments 1–5 of Part 10 are all achievable within one revision cycle with the authors' existing tooling; with them, plus the internal-consistency repairs, this becomes an acceptable resource paper. Without them, the contributions rest on demonstration rather than evidence.
