Introduction back to ToC

Nepal's cultural heritage is overwhelmingly *living*: temples are not merely monuments but active ritual sites, deities are periodically re-consecrated and processioned, and stewardship is mediated by centuries-old socio-religious trusts called *Guthi*. Documenting this heritage requires more than a catalogue of objects — it requires modelling the **events**, **agents**, **time-spans**, and **sources** that give the objects their meaning, and doing so in a way that records *who said what, on what basis*. ### Motivation General cultural-heritage models capture objects and their attributes well, but they struggle with three features that are central to Nepalese living heritage: 1. **Event-centric ritual practice** — the same shrine participates in recurring festivals, chariot processions, masked dances, and the selection and retirement of living goddesses (*Kumari*); these are temporal, sequenced activities, not static properties. 2. **Institutional stewardship** — *Guthi* trusts mediate the relationship between temples, rituals, land, and communities and must be first-class entities. 3. **Contested, multi-source knowledge** — construction dates, attributions, and syncretic Hindu–Buddhist identities are frequently disputed across oral histories, archival records, and field surveys, so the model must keep assertion-level provenance and epistemic stance. ### State of the art HeritageGraph builds on, and stays interoperable with, the established standards of the domain: **CIDOC‑CRM** (event-centric documentation), **CRMinf** (argumentation and belief), **PROV‑O** (provenance), and **Europeana EDM** (aggregation and publication). It complements CIDOC-CRM extensions such as CRMba and CRMsoc by adding the Nepal-specific notions of Guthi-mediated stewardship, ritual activation of sacred space, multi-calendar dating, and multi-source reconciliation. ### Goals The ontology is designed to answer concrete **competency questions** across four dimensions — *structural* (architecture, typology, location), *ritual/festival* (activities, sequences, materials), *institutional/syncretic* (Guthi, multi-tradition relationships), and *living-goddess* (Kumari selection, tenure, retirement) — while remaining a clean, reusable OWL vocabulary published under a permanent `https://w3id.org/heritagegraph/` identifier.
